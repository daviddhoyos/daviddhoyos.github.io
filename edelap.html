Case study

Optimizing Essential Services for Millions

I redesigned the customer virtual office of Edelap, an electricity provider in Argentina, as a mobile app. I reworked the billing and payment flows so paying a bill is the first thing users see.

  • Product Design
  • UX/UI Design
  • Research
Role
Product Designer
Methods
Heatmaps, card sorting, heuristic review, interviews

From a button with 1% of the attention to the main action

In the original virtual office, “Pagar ahora” (Pay now) received about 1% of user attention. In the redesign, the balance and the payment button are the first things on the home screen.

The Challenge

The old virtual office had usability and accessibility problems. Before redesigning it, I wanted to know where users got stuck, how to measure it and which fixes would matter most.

  • What are the critical friction points in the current user journey?
  • How can we accurately measure the impact of these inefficiencies?
  • Where should we focus our design efforts to drive the most value?

Interviews

The discovered Insights are valuable for improving the current product, for future versions or new services.

What I found

Each problem pointed to a design opportunity:

  1. Problem: There is no consistency in the information architecture.

    Opportunity: Organize the menu by prioritizing the most used actions.

  2. Problem: Doesn’t follow UI design pattern standards.

    Opportunity: Provide clarity to the main CTA

  3. Problem: Information in a language unfamiliar to the user.

    Opportunity: Write text content employing a more user-friendly language.

  4. Problem: There is no hierarchy of typography, colors or shapes.

    Opportunity: Use of Color Theory, Gestalt, UX Writing

Components

By grounding the Information Architecture in user insights, I removed the guesswork from the UI phase and carried those decisions into a scalable component library.

Fewer choices in the main navigation

The old virtual office had 11 items in its main menu. I cut it to four, Inicio, Facturas, Reporte and Ayuda (Home, Bills, Report, Help), based on the card sorting results. Address and supply settings moved into the profile, since users rarely need them. Fewer options means users find what they need faster (Hick's Law).

  • Nested Settings: Address and supply management are nested within the profile to declutter the main interface.

  • Primary Focus: The balance due is the clear visual focal point, supported by helpers for immediate status clarity.

  • Ergonomic CTA: A full-width button placed right below the balance: a large, predictable target that is quick to reach and tap (Fitts’s Law).

  • Balanced Insights: Home screen shows a high-level YoY comparison, linking to detailed analytics for power users.

  • High User Satisfaction: Driven by a friction-free and intuitive mobile journey.
  • Increased Adoption: Streamlined access led to higher engagement with digital services.
  • Inclusive Design: Built toward WCAG 2.1 AA, with accessible contrast, readable type sizes and large touch targets.
  • Reduced Friction: Paying a bill went from a hidden action to the main action on the home screen.

Looking back

  • What worked

    Combining heatmaps with card sorting showed both where users looked and how they expected the menu to be organized.

  • What I’d do differently

    Validate the new navigation with a tree test before moving to high-fidelity screens.

  • What I’d measure next

    Bill payment completion rate and time to pay, tracked after launch.
