Rebrand and website for Propelify Innovation Festival

An identity for the “SXSW of the Northeast”

Propelify is TechUnited:NJ’s innovation festival on the Hudson waterfront. Its site was coral and blue, and its home was 2.6 times longer than TechCrunch Disrupt’s. I led the rebrand and redesigned the site, from the home to registration and tickets, and carried the identity onto the festival’s signage. The system stayed in use for more than three years.

Role
Lead designer, rebrand and website
Client
TechUnited:NJ
Scope
Identity, information architecture, website, registration, tickets, signage
In use

At a glance

The problem

A home that tried to say everything at once, in colors that didn’t feel like a tech festival.

What I did

A new identity, one path per audience, forms for exhibitors, speakers and sponsors, a two-step checkout, and signage.

The outcome

The festival ran on the system for more than three years, through its 2024 ticket page.

The business

A festival that sells a community

TechUnited:NJ is a non-profit, membership-driven organization that helps New Jersey’s tech companies and founders connect and grow. Propelify is its biggest stage.

Since 2016 the festival has brought more than 40,000 people to the waterfront facing Manhattan, along with hundreds of exhibitors and speakers like Arianna Huffington and Gary Vaynerchuk. Each edition also hosts a startup competition with $100,000 in cash prizes.

The festival is how TechUnited:NJ builds its brand and its influence. For one day it puts the organization at the center of New Jersey’s tech community, connecting founders with investors and companies, and that position gives it a voice with sponsors and partners for the rest of the year. Tickets, booths and sponsorships pay for the event, and the members and partners it brings in keep the organization going.

For a one-day event, the website does most of the selling: it turns curiosity into a ticket, and convinces exhibitors, speakers and sponsors that the right crowd will be there.

For design, that meant serving four decisions at once: buying a ticket, booking a booth, applying to speak and sponsoring. Each one needed its own path from the header, and none of them could bury the others.

The starting point

Everything on one page, in coral and blue

I benchmarked the old site against two festivals Propelify competes with for attention, Collision and TechCrunch Disrupt. Propelify’s home was 2.6 times longer than TechCrunch’s: every speaker, sponsor, activity and logistics note stacked on one page, under a busy pattern of icons. Forbes was already calling Propelify the “SXSW of the Northeast”, and the site looked like a local fair. The brand had to catch up with the reputation, especially for the sponsors deciding where to put their event budget.

The idea

A rocket for a festival named after propelling ideas

The name already had the concept: propel. The identity turned it into a rocket drawn in low-poly lines, on a near-black base that feels like an evening event, with a hot pink for actions and a gradient from electric blue to red-pink. Type is Kanit throughout.

Who it’s for

Four crowds, four ways in

The festival brings together people who come for different reasons. The site kept the four paths the old one had, Attend, Exhibit, Speak and Sponsor, and gave each one a page that ends in its own form.

  • Attend

    Founders, investors and tech talent. They get a ticket page with three tiers and a two-step checkout.

  • Exhibit

    Startups and companies that want a booth. They register through their own form.

  • Speak

    People who want the stage. Their application asks for format, topic and experience.

  • Sponsor

    Brands and partners, from investors to media. Their application asks which one they are.

Information architecture

Audiences up front, logistics one level down

The header holds the four audiences and Register now. Everything a visitor needs once they’ve decided to go moved to a second level and the footer, with its own page each, instead of living on the home.

The home’s job

From “what is this?” to “I’m going” in one screen

A first-time visitor needs four answers before registering. The home answers all of them above the video.

  1. What is it?

    The name and “Innovation Festival” under it, set as one lockup.

  2. Is it worth a day?

    A line from Forbes, from someone other than the organizers.

  3. When and where?

    Date and venue, right under the button.

  4. How do I go?

    Register now: the only filled button, in the brand’s pink.

Tickets and forms

Where the site makes its money

Tickets come in three tiers, so the difference between them reads in a glance. Buying one takes two steps: choose your tickets and fill in billing, then review the order and pay, with a coupon field and an optional drink voucher on the way.

Off screen

The same rocket, from the browser to the festival grounds

It started as a website redesign and ended close to a full rebrand. TechUnited took the identity onto merchandise and dressed the festival itself in the website’s colors. Every sign ends in the same blue-to-pink wave, so speaker areas, sponsor boards and partner thank-yous read as one family.

Looking back

What worked

Giving the name a shape. A rocket built from triangles worked as a logo, a pattern, an illustration style and a sign system, which is why it lasted beyond the website and stayed in use for more than three years.

What I missed

Sponsors are one of a festival’s main sources of money, yet the Sponsor path ended in an application form. A company deciding where to spend its event budget wants three answers before it writes to anyone: who it will reach, what it gets, and why paying for a higher tier is worth it. The page should sell, with the form at the end.

How that page could work

  • Show the audience first

    The numbers a marketing team reports upwards: more than 40,000 attendees since 2016, and the mix of founders, investors and corporate innovators in the room.

  • Make each tier add something you can see

    Entry levels buy visibility, a logo on the site and the signs. Middle levels add presence, a booth and passes for the team. The top adds a voice: a stage, a speaking slot, access to the Investor Lounge.

  • Let the top tier do the selling

    Compared side by side, the jump between tiers should read as small next to what it adds. That keeps the middle tier attractive and makes the top one an easy case to take to a budget meeting.
