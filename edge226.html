Website redesign and rebrand for Edge226

Explaining ad tech to the people who buy it

Edge226 is a multichannel performance marketing platform: mobile apps and games pay it for installs and purchases, not for impressions. Its website never said so. Working with Edge226’s marketing lead, I redesigned every page, and what started as a website project became the company’s new brand.

At a glance

The problem

Eight menu items named after internal teams, and not one line about what set Edge226 apart: clients pay for results.

What I did

Turned marketing’s copy into pages, rebuilt the information architecture twice, and wrote a style guide for desktop and mobile.

The outcome

The redesign became the rebrand. Edge226 moved from blue to violet and used the identity for about four years, online and at its event booths.

The business

A company that gets paid when its clients grow

Edge226 is an ad-tech company founded in Israel, with offices in Tel Aviv and New York. Its core product, Peak, is a demand-side platform: software that buys ad space across thousands of apps on behalf of advertisers, and uses machine learning to decide where each dollar goes.

Its clients are mobile apps and games looking for users who stay and pay, in verticals like gaming, finance, travel and food delivery. Since then it has grown into connected TV and rewarded ads, and in 2026 it acquired AnyClip, an AI video company.

The vocabulary behind the site

DSP
Demand-side platform. Buys ad space automatically for advertisers, across many apps at once.
Mobile supply
The ad space available inside apps. The more of it a DSP reaches, the more users it can find.
Outcome-based pricing
The advertiser pays for a result, such as an install or a purchase, instead of for each time an ad is shown.
ROAS
Return on ad spend: revenue earned for every dollar spent on ads. The number a growth team is judged by.
Vertical
An app category, like finance or gaming, with its own goals and its own idea of a valuable user.

How the money moves

The business model is the website’s whole argument

Most ad platforms charge for reach. Edge226 charges for results. That difference is what a visitor needs to get before anything else on the page matters.

  1. An app sets a goal

    Installs, deposits, subscriptions or purchases: the action that is worth money to that business.

  2. The platform bids for space

    Its algorithms buy placements across in-app inventory, looking for the users most likely to act.

  3. A user acts

    Someone sees the ad, installs the app and, ideally, keeps using it.

  4. The app pays for the outcome

    Not for the impression. Spend is tied to the goal set in step one.

The starting point

A site organized around the company, not the buyer

The original site was blue and cyan, with a robot in the hero and eight items in the menu: User Acquisition, Technology, Our Approach, Resources, Creative Studio, Careers, About Us and Contact Us. A marketer had to know Edge226’s internal structure to find what it sold, and pricing by results appeared only as a line in a paragraph.

The idea

Named after a triathlon

226 kilometers is the full triathlon distance: 3.8 swimming, 180 cycling, 42.2 running. The name was about endurance and performance, and the product was already called Peak.

So the visual language climbs. Peak’s three pillars, guaranteed results, global transparent supply and performance through technology, sit around a summit on the home. The Peak page opens with a mountain in the brand gradient. Every metaphor points the same way: up, toward the result the client pays for.

How we worked

Marketing brought the message. I turned it into pages.

Edge226’s marketing lead was my counterpart for the whole project. Each page went through the same loop until it said what the company needed it to say.

  1. Values and copy

    Marketing defined what Edge226 stands for and what each page had to communicate, and handed over the copy.

  2. Copy into structure

    I turned that copy into layouts: what each section says first, what proves it, and where the visitor goes next.

  3. Rounds of feedback

    Every version went back to the client. Feedback was mostly positive, sometimes a clear no, and each no sent a page back for another pass.

  4. Moving without answers

    Replies could take days, and the project couldn’t stop. Keeping the work moving between rounds was part of the job.

Solutions and Verticals, side by side. What Edge226 sells, and the industries it sells to, each one click from the header. One way to ask for a demo, and a login kept apart for existing clients.

Why Verticals got a place in the menu

  • Each industry counts a different result

    A finance app counts deposits, a game counts players who pay, a delivery app counts repeat orders. A page per vertical talks about the number that buyer is judged by.

  • Buyers look for their own market first

    A marketer trusts a platform that already shows it knows apps like theirs. Putting industries next to Solutions put that proof one click from the header, where the decision to book a demo starts.

Color as wayfinding

A gradient for each part of the site

The sitemap assigned every section its own blend of the same three colors, blue #4158D0, magenta #C850C0 and peach #FFCC70, so visitors can tell where they are from the header alone.

The style guide

One system for every page, on every screen

The guide covered color, type, buttons, graphic resources and how each page adapts to mobile. On a phone, the hero keeps one job: get to the demo.

A closer look

Outcome first, product second

The Global Scale page shows the approach: talk about what the buyer wants, then prove the platform can deliver it.

  1. 1

    Marker 1: One filled button

    Login, for current clients, stays outlined. Book a Demo is the only filled button in the header, so new visitors always know the next step.

  2. 2

    Marker 2: The buyer’s goal as the headline

    “Reach a global customer base that’s right for your app” speaks to the result. Peak is named in the subhead, next to its two promises: scale and transparency.

  3. 3

    Marker 3: Proof in three numbers

    99% of the world’s mobile supply, 4 billion devices, 2M+ applications answer “how big is your reach?” before anyone asks.

From website to brand

The redesign became the rebrand

It started as a new website. By the end, Edge226 had adopted the new palette as its identity: the company went from blue to violet, and kept the look for about four years, on its site and on the banners it took to industry events.

BeforeCyan and navy
AfterBlue into violet and magenta

Still there today

The site has been rebuilt since. The structure hasn’t changed.

Edge226’s current website (2026) has new visuals and new products, and keeps the decisions underneath.

  • The same four sections

    The header still leads with Solutions, Verticals, Resources and Company, now holding CTV and rewarded ads next to in-app.

  • The same main action

    Book a Demo is still the primary button, with Login kept for existing clients.

  • The same proof point

    Reaching 99% of global mobile supply is still one of the first claims a visitor reads.

Looking back

What I learned

We wanted different colors, but the client had already fallen in love with the gradients. Later we saw how hard they were to implement and scale to other screens. To keep pages light and accessible, I used them as accents instead of primary colors.

The lesson: decide carefully before presenting something to a client, so they don’t fall in love with an idea that won’t scale. And explain the reasoning well enough to negotiate. This project taught me to negotiate.

What I missed

The vertical pages explained Edge226 to each industry, but results from real clients in that industry lived in Resources, one click away from where a finance marketer was making up their mind. Today I’d put at least one client result on every vertical page.
